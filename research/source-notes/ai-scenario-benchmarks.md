# AI Scenario Benchmarks for AETHER

Last updated: 2026-08-09

## Purpose

AETHER assumes a future in which AI and robotics can accelerate scientific and physical work, but it must not smuggle that premise into the engineering model as if an AI scenario were measured industrial evidence. This note compares three prominent scenario projects and records the narrower ways they may be used.

## Source classification

### Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead

Leopold Aschenbrenner's June 2024 essay extrapolates compute, algorithmic efficiency, and the removal of practical capability constraints toward AGI around 2027 and superintelligence by the end of the decade. Its most useful AETHER contribution is not the date. It is the distinction between cognitive acceleration and physical deployment: AI research can be automated in a virtual environment before robotics catches up, while compute and electrical power remain binding industrial inputs.

AETHER use: a hard and fast capability stress branch and an industrial-mobilization comparator. Do not use it as evidence for robot task-hours, construction productivity, capture energy, or storage throughput.

### AI 2027

AI 2027, published April 3, 2025 by Daniel Kokotajlo, Scott Alexander, Thomas Larsen, Eli Lifland, and Romeo Dean, is a detailed forecast scenario. At publication it represented the authors' best guess, informed by trend extrapolation, wargames, expert feedback, and tabletop exercises. The authors also state that exact timing is uncertain and that uncertainty expands substantially beyond the near-term portion of the scenario.

AETHER use: a short-timeline branch in which software R&D acceleration arrives before 2030. It can test how little time physical institutions would have to adapt. It cannot establish the productivity, reliability, or supply-chain completeness of robots operating climate infrastructure.

### AI 2040: Plan A

AI 2040: Plan A, by Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt, and Daniel Kokotajlo, is primarily a recommendation expressed as a scenario. Its default path has automated AI R&D in 2030, a governed pause at roughly top-human-expert capability in 2035, and superintelligence in 2040. Its economics material explicitly separates cognitive labor from physical labor and makes robot abundance conditional on automated supply chains, energy, and infrastructure.

AETHER use: a governance-bounded abundance branch. It is useful for testing international coordination, controlled diffusion, and a slower physical transition. Its robot and energy assumptions remain scenario inputs, not empirical validation.

## Comparison rule

These projects widen the plausible timing and governance envelope. They do not source AETHER's engineering parameters. Capture energy, clean-power deliverability, robot field productivity, materials, storage, lifecycle emissions, MRV, and rebound remain independent feasibility gates.
