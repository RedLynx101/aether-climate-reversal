# Clean-Energy Market and Firm-Power Notes

Last updated: 2026-06-09

AETHER should not assume clean energy scales only through command-and-control regulation. The cost curve increasingly does some of the work. The best version of the argument is not "regulation is unnecessary." It is more specific: if clean electricity is cheaper over the asset life, markets can pull it into the system, while public policy should focus on clearing bottlenecks, pricing externalities, and preventing rebound rather than micromanaging every deployment decision.

## Cost-Driven Deployment

IRENA reports that in 2024 new utility-scale onshore wind had a global weighted-average LCOE of $0.034/kWh and solar PV averaged $0.043/kWh. IRENA also reports that 91% of newly commissioned utility-scale renewable capacity delivered power below the cost of the cheapest new fossil alternative, and that utility-scale battery storage costs fell to $192/kWh, down 93% since 2010.

Source key: `irena_power_costs_2024`

Implication for AETHER: the clean-energy buildout can be partly market-pulled if the system is allowed to connect cheap resources. The hard constraints shift toward grid interconnection, transmission, storage, land, finance, firm power, and industrial siting.

## Texas and California

Texas and California show different clean-energy deployment models.

Texas is not usually framed as a heavy climate-regulation state, yet EIA reports that Texas led U.S. wind generation in 2024, accounting for about 28% of the U.S. total, and was second in solar generation after California. This is not pure laissez-faire; Texas benefited from resource quality, ERCOT market structure, federal incentives, and earlier transmission planning such as Competitive Renewable Energy Zones. Still, it is evidence that clean energy can win in a market-led environment when the economics and grid path work.

California is more policy-driven and bureaucratic, but the California Energy Commission reports that clean energy resources accounted for 62% of California's total system power mix in 2024. California shows that policy can force a high clean share, but it can also add process cost, siting friction, and administrative complexity.

Implication for AETHER: use a market-compatible framing. Cheap clean energy should be allowed to outcompete fossil generation where it can. Policy should make the market honest: open interconnection, build transmission, price emissions, protect reliability, speed nuclear licensing where safe, and stop fake offsets.

## Grid and Interconnection Bottlenecks

Market demand alone does not connect power plants to loads. Berkeley Lab's `Queued Up: 2025 Edition` documents very large U.S. interconnection queues, dominated by solar, storage, and wind. That matters for AETHER because a cheap project waiting years in a queue is not an electron available for carbon removal.

Implication for AETHER: a serious proposal should treat transmission and interconnection reform as climate infrastructure. This is not "more bureaucracy." It is a requirement to let cheaper resources actually reach demand.

## Nuclear, Fusion, and Data Centers

Data centers are creating early market pull for clean firm power. IEA projects data centre electricity consumption roughly doubling to about 945-950 TWh by 2030. IEA also says renewables remain the fastest-growing source for data centres, while nuclear power becomes more significant in the United States after 2030 as the first SMRs are expected.

Concrete signals:

- Microsoft and Constellation announced a 20-year PPA to restart TMI Unit 1 as the Crane Clean Energy Center, adding about 835 MW of carbon-free power to the grid.
- Google and Kairos Power announced an agreement to deploy up to 500 MW of advanced nuclear by 2035, with first deployment targeted by 2030.
- Amazon bought a data-center campus adjacent to Talen's Susquehanna nuclear plant and other Amazon/X-energy efforts point to nuclear as a firm-power strategy.
- Helion announced a Microsoft fusion PPA targeting at least 50 MW and electricity by 2028. Treat this as high-upside and high-risk.
- The Fusion Industry Association reported $2.64B in fusion investment in the prior year and $9.7B cumulative private funding since 2021.

Implication for AETHER: nuclear and fusion should not be the base case for 100 GtCO2/year removal by themselves. Current deals are measured in tens or hundreds of megawatts, while AETHER needs terawatts. But the data-center boom is valuable because it creates customers willing to pay for clean firm power, which could mature supply chains, licensing, financing, and modular construction before AETHER needs them.

## Clean-Energy Buildout Sensitivity

At 100 GtCO2/year:

- 1 GJ/tCO2 needs 27,778 TWh/year.
- 3 GJ/tCO2 needs 83,333 TWh/year.
- 8 GJ/tCO2 needs 222,222 TWh/year.
- 11.94 GJ/tCO2 needs 331,708 TWh/year.

IEA reports global electricity generation increased by more than 850 TWh in 2025, with solar PV alone adding about 600 TWh. If annual clean-generation additions start at 850 TWh/year and keep growing:

| Annual growth in clean-generation additions | Cumulative new annual generation after 20 years | AETHER 3 GJ/tCO2 coverage before ordinary demand |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 17,000 TWh/year | 20% |
| 5% | 28,106 TWh/year | 34% |
| 10% | 48,684 TWh/year | 58% |
| 15% | 87,077 TWh/year | 104% |
| 20% | 158,685 TWh/year | 190% |

This is the clean-energy crux. The advanced 3 GJ/tCO2 AETHER case becomes arithmetically possible over 20 years if clean-generation additions keep compounding at roughly 15% annually from today's already-large base. But ordinary electrification, data centers, industrial growth, and replacement of fossil generation will claim a large share of those additions. AETHER therefore needs both clean-energy abundance and a deployment architecture that uses power where it is cheapest, cleanest, and least grid-constrained.
