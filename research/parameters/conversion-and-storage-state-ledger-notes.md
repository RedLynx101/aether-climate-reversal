# Conversion and Storage-State Ledger Notes

Last updated: 2026-06-09

This note makes the physical conversions explicit. The purpose is to prevent a common mistake in climate-reversal thinking: treating CO2 as a single interchangeable object after capture. The storage state changes volume, safety burden, monitoring, and energy cost.

## 100 GtCO2/year Ledger

| State or product | Mass | Volume | Added energy | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| CO2 gas at STP | 100 Gt/year | 50505.1 km3/year | pathway-specific | Too bulky for serious planetary storage; useful as a contrast case. |
| Supercritical CO2 | 100 Gt/year | 166.7 km3/year | pathway-specific | Far denser than gas, but constrained by geology, injection rates, pressure, liability, and monitoring. |
| Solid carbon from full splitting | 27.292 Gt/year | 12.41 km3/year | 248,333 TWh/year | Compact, but the energy and product-handling burden makes it a special case rather than the default. |
| Liquid O2 coproduct from full splitting | 72.707 Gt/year | 63.72 km3/year | 248,333 TWh/year | O2 should not be treated as a harmless afterthought; local oxygen handling hazards matter even if atmospheric release is the endpoint. |
| Magnesite-equivalent mineral carbonate | 191.579 Gt/year | 63.86 km3/year | pathway-specific | Durable and dense, but constrained by reactive minerals, mining, grinding, transport, water, kinetics, and local impacts. |

## Key Read

At 100 GtCO2/year, gas-phase CO2 is not a credible storage state. Supercritical CO2 is compact enough to be physically imaginable, but the bottleneck becomes geology, injection rate, pressure management, monitoring, liability, and public acceptance.

Full splitting changes the storage problem but does not remove the scale problem. It produces about 27.3 Gt/year of solid carbon and about 72.7 Gt/year of O2. The solid carbon volume is only about 12.4 km3/year, but the ideal splitting burden is about 248,333 TWh/year before real electrochemical losses. The annual carbon product would be about 15,162x current world natural graphite mine production and about 34.1x reported recoverable natural graphite resources. AETHER can manufacture carbon rather than mine natural graphite, but the comparison shows why solid-carbon handling cannot be waved away.

The magnesite-equivalent mineral carbonate proxy is dense and durable, but it creates about 191.6 Gt/year of solid carbonate product for 100 GtCO2/year. That pushes the problem into mining, grinding, transport, kinetics, water, land disturbance, and local ecological constraints.

The current conclusion is unchanged but better grounded: AETHER should default toward geologic storage, in situ mineralization, and pathway-specific durable storage where the local resource base can support it. CO2 splitting is a special-purpose option for high-value carbon products, remote stranded-energy systems, or cases where the storage and MRV benefits justify the energy cost.
