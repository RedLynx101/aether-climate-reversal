# AETHER Research Backlog

Project title: AETHER: Atmospheric Engineering Through High-Energy Removal

## Carbon and Energy Conversions

- Verify conversion from CO2 mass to carbon mass: 44 tonnes CO2 contains 12 tonnes carbon.
- Verify conversion from tonnes CO2 to atmospheric ppm. Use `1 ppm ~= 7.8 GtCO2 in air` as a starting value, then model carbon-cycle rebound.
- Estimate theoretical minimum energy for separating CO2 from air.
- Estimate theoretical minimum energy for splitting CO2 into carbon and oxygen.
- Compare theoretical minimums with practical process energy.
- Separate electricity, heat, compression, transport, injection, monitoring, and replacement energy.

## Removal Pathways

- Direct air capture: cost, energy intensity, sorbent lifetime, water use, land use, heat requirements, storage pairing.
- Enhanced weathering: rock type, mining energy, grinding energy, spreading logistics, measurement uncertainty, ecological risk.
- Mineralization: feedstock availability, reaction kinetics, grinding cost, carbonate durability, mine-tailings use.
- Biochar: feedstock limits, permanence, soil effects, pyrolysis energy, monitoring.
- Bio-oil storage: conversion cost, underground injection constraints, permanence, leakage.
- BECCS: biomass supply, land competition, food and biodiversity tradeoffs.
- Ocean alkalinity and direct ocean capture: chemistry, MRV, ecological risk, governance.
- Point-source capture: relevance to cement, steel, lime, waste, and residual industrial emissions.

## Storage State Comparisons

For each storage form, collect:

- Density.
- Energy cost to create and maintain that state.
- Storage capacity.
- Leakage risk.
- Monitoring burden.
- Expected permanence.
- Liability structure.
- Transport requirements.
- Local environmental risk.

Priority forms:

- Gaseous CO2.
- Liquid CO2.
- Supercritical CO2.
- Mineral carbonate.
- Solid carbon.
- Biochar.
- Bio-oil.
- Dissolved ocean bicarbonate/carbonate.

## Robotics and Automation

- Robot unit cost by platform.
- Robot-as-a-service price by platform.
- Useful life and duty cycle.
- Maintenance and integration cost.
- Payload, speed, precision, autonomy, and supervision requirements.
- Manufacturing ramp rates and supply-chain constraints.
- Energy use per operating hour.
- Failure rates and downtime.
- Safety and liability constraints.
- Which tasks are better done by humanoids versus specialized machines.

## Scenario Design

- Conservative: slow CDR deployment, limited robotics gains, high energy costs.
- Base: continued clean-energy cost decline, moderate automation, steady CDR learning.
- Aggressive: rapid robotics deployment, cheap clean electricity, durable CDR learning curves.
- Failure case: cheap deployment with weak MRV, fake credits, or non-durable storage.

## Governance and MRV

- Credit durability classes.
- Double-counting prevention.
- Long-term liability for leakage.
- Third-party audits.
- Sensor networks and satellite monitoring.
- Tamper-resistant project accounting.
- Policy designs that reward real tonnes, not paper tonnes.
- Commons ownership and citizen-dividend path: explore whether lakes, oceans, and the atmosphere can be treated as citizen-owned or trust-held commons, where emitters pay for permitted outputs at rates tied to quantity/risk while dangerous outputs remain restricted. See `research/open-questions/commons-ownership-and-citizen-dividends.md`.
