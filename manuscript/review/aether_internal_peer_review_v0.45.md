# AETHER internal peer-review pass: v0.45

Reviewer: OpenAI Codex, acting as an internal critical reviewer rather than an independent external or journal peer reviewer  
Review date: 2026-08-10  
Project originator and principal author: Noah Hicks

## Overall judgment

AETHER remains legitimate to share as a conditional feasibility working paper and open research program. The v0.45 framing is more coherent than the previous mitigation-versus-removal language because it identifies an actual operating model: a publicly governed atmospheric range, a net carbon budget, measured use, a system charge, and removal procurement. It is not yet legitimate to present the public-carbon-utility model as established law, a calibrated tariff, or a demonstrated institution.

## What this pass checked

This pass reviewed the full manuscript change, public website, repository framing, video plan, and generated-paper pipeline for consistency. It checked whether assumptions are labeled, whether the proposed accounting equations are dimensionally intelligible, whether the new governance claim overstates present legal authority, and whether the revised prose still preserves the physical meaning of the existing scenario calculations.

## Findings resolved in v0.45

1. The paper now treats useful industrial CO2 output as a manageable system load rather than a moral category. Net use still has to fit inside a measured atmospheric budget.
2. Public ownership is stated as AETHER's proposed institutional model, not current property law.
3. The governance section now defines a physical net-use account before discussing prices. Additions and retired durable removals use the same tonne-of-CO2-per-year accounting unit.
4. The conceptual tariff separates marginal removal, storage/MRV/liability, and scarcity-and-risk components. It is explicitly a design equation rather than a numerical price estimate.
5. Revenue uses are named: removal, transport, storage, verification, maintenance, liability reserves, affected communities, and possible citizen dividends.
6. The paper prevents a carbon payment from overriding local pollution law. Toxic co-pollutants and acute harms remain subject to separate restrictions and liability.
7. Rebound is reframed as induced demand outrunning verified capacity. The underlying 57.8%, 41.7%, and 23.1% accounting thresholds are unchanged and remain traceable to the independent calculation audit.
8. The public site no longer advertises a video that has not been made, and the rejected design routes and assets have been removed.

## Assumptions that remain open

- What legal instrument could hold an atmospheric service for citizens across jurisdictions.
- Who sets the operating band and how regional, national, and global budgets reconcile.
- Whether the charge is a tax, tariff, auction price, permit obligation, procurement settlement, or hybrid.
- How scarcity, uncertainty, storage liability, and distributional effects enter a calibrated price.
- Whether and how firms can retire removals against their account without recreating a weak offset market.
- How revenue is divided among system investment, affected communities, liability reserves, and dividends.
- How local environmental burdens, trade exposure, border adjustments, evasion, and regulatory capture are handled.

## Calculation read

The new governance equations are meaningful as a dimensional ledger, not as an empirical calculation. `verified additions - retired verified durable removals` produces net tonnes of CO2. Multiplying positive net tonnes by a charge in dollars per tonne produces an annual dollar obligation. The equation should not be used to quote a tariff until the cost, risk, scarcity, and incidence terms are modeled. No existing physical or climate-model output was changed by this framing revision.

## Remaining public-release boundary

Share v0.45 as a proposal for criticism. The strongest invitation is: test the physical gates and help design an institution that allows socially useful carbon use without letting net atmospheric load exceed verified capacity. External review is still needed from climate-cycle, CDR, power, storage, MRV, robotics, economics, public-finance, administrative-law, environmental-justice, and international-governance specialists.
