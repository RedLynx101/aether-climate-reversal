# AETHER evidence and review readiness

**Current revision:** v0.46, September 2026
**Status:** Conditional research program. Internal technical and editorial review completed; no external peer review, field validation, or engineering certification.

## What is ready for review

The current paper makes a narrow, inspectable claim: an accountable public-carbon service and a regional ordinary-versus-automation analytical benchmark are worth testing under explicit physical, financial, and institutional constraints. Reviewers can inspect the source register, model inputs and outputs, the correction record, and the current manuscript.

- Current principal paper: `manuscript/paper/aether_scientific_paper.md`
- Technical inventory: `manuscript/paper/technical_supplement.md`

- Carbon-baseline artifacts: `data/carbon-baseline/` and updated carbon tables
- Physical-accounting outputs: regional dispatch, lifecycle, storage, and MRV summary tables

## What the package does not establish

It does not establish that 100 GtCO2/year is viable, that a 1 MtCO2/year facility should be built, that automation will achieve a stated productivity, or that a specific atmospheric concentration is optimal. The 100 GtCO2/year figure is a gross stress test. The corrected representative-day dispatch result is not an 8760-hour power result. The first RCMIP/Joos absolute-output method is quarantined after an implausible zero-future-emissions diagnostic. The integrated model is partially coupled screening, not a common compatible deployment scenario.

Monte Carlo pass shares are not probabilities. Lifecycle/MRV results are scalar accounting screens, not species- and time-resolved climate results. Cost floors and the initial learning state remain stated scenario inputs.

## Best external-review packet

Ask each reviewer one bounded falsification question:

1. **Carbon cycle:** Does the paired reference-and-removal construction isolate the marginal effect of removal, and what would a minimally credible upgrade require?
2. **Process and power:** Does the 1 MtCO2/year case distinguish electricity, heat, storage, and ordinary demand under a defensible regional boundary?
3. **Storage and MRV:** Are injection rate, monitoring, reversal liability, and credit buffers represented in the right order and at useful resolution?
4. **Automation:** Does ordinary-versus-automation comparison isolate a measurable operational mechanism under the same physical system?
5. **Finance and governance:** Does the stock-flow design remain solvent through legacy drawdown, under-delivery, and declining emissions-fee revenue?

The appropriate response to a failed review gate is to narrow or withdraw the affected claim.
