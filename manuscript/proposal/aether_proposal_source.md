## Abstract

AETHER proposes research on an accountable carbon-removal service: a system that could pair useful industrial activity with atmospheric-stock management, prevent avoidable releases, procure the best available durable interventions for residual emissions, and carry storage and reversal obligations beyond the life of an individual operator. It does not assume that CO2 is morally bad or that direct air capture should replace prevention, concentrated-source capture, process change, or demand reduction. The relevant comparison is delivered physical and lifecycle performance under a clear liability boundary.

The old AETHER 100 GtCO2/year model package was useful as a collection of stress tests but was not a validated coupled forecast. This proposal retains 100 GtCO2/year only as an extreme gross-removal screen and centers the next phase on one reproducible 1 MtCO2/year regional analytical case. That case will compare ordinary and automation-assisted execution under the same removal process, power and heat specification, storage route, and measurement boundary. It will identify mechanisms of improvement rather than assuming that AI or robotics changes physical constraints. The research is conditional feasibility work, not a deployment plan.

## 1. Problem

Atmospheric CO2 remains elevated and emissions remain large ([NOAA GML, 2026](https://www.gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/global.html); [Friedlingstein et al., 2026](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/3211/2026/)). Current novel CDR is tiny relative to the industrial scales often discussed ([State of CDR, 2026](https://www.stateofcdr.org/report/3rd-edition)). A carbon service cannot change this by announcing capacity. It needs a coherent physical ledger, a power and storage plan, reliable measurement, and a financial structure that remains solvent when emissions-fee revenue declines.

The proposal's hypothesis is narrow: automation may improve named physical and operational bottlenecks in carbon-management infrastructure, such as design iteration, construction schedule, maintenance, drilling support, monitoring, and plant availability. It does not lower thermodynamic limits by itself or provide evidence that a general AI timeline produces a stated removal rate.

## 2. Accounting boundary

The program reports four quantities separately: gross capture, durable physical removal after separately stated lifecycle debits, creditable removal after measurement and reversal buffers, and the net climate result after residual and induced emissions. A credit is not a tonne physically retained, and neither is a climate forecast.

Electricity, useful heat at its required temperature, and chemical energy remain distinct. A scalar annual lifecycle ledger is not a time- and species-resolved climate calculation. These distinctions are material when comparing DAC, point-source capture, mineralization, and other pathways.

## 3. Why the earlier headline is being narrowed

100 GtCO2/year remains a useful stress test because it exposes orders of magnitude. At 1--3 GJ/tCO2, capture alone implies roughly 27,800--83,300 TWh/year. Those conversions do not predict that the energy intensity or buildout will occur. The IPCC and the DAC literature identify energy, materials, water, storage, and governance constraints that cannot be bypassed by a cost curve ([IPCC, 2022](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-12/); [Chatterjee & Huang, 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17203-7); [Young et al., 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590332223003007)).

An audit found that the legacy representative-day dispatch model repeatedly annualized initial battery energy. Requiring cyclic storage reduced the named upper-tail synthetic case from 121.97 to approximately 98.40 GtCO2/year. This is a conservation diagnostic using the same synthetic profiles, not a weather-resolved annual model. The former carbon calculation also lacked a historically initialized evolving baseline. These corrections withdraw the affected old results from use as quantitative evidence.

## 4. Proposed regional analytical case

The first proposed result is a 1 MtCO2/year regional case with a named removal process, low-carbon electricity and temperature-specific heat source, transport and storage corridor, MRV plan, lifecycle ledger, and financial stock-flow. It compares ordinary execution with automation assistance under the same physical conditions. Using NETL's solvent DAC Case 1 as a process anchor ([NETL, 2022](https://www.netl.doe.gov/projects/files/DirectAirCaptureCaseStudiesSolventSystem_083122.pdf)), its stated inputs are 0.533129 MWh/tCO2 electricity and 6.846332 GJ/tCO2 heat.

In the matched analytical mechanism test, ordinary operations capture 850,000 gross tonnes/year and automation-assisted operations capture 900,000. Assumed uptime changes from 0.85 to 0.90; task hours change from 660,000 to 345,000/year, and automation adds a $12 million system cost at the same $85/hour task cost. Retained stored CO2 is 843,214.4 and 892,815.3 tCO2/year, then the stated project-emissions debit yields 798,123.7 and 845,072.2 tCO2e/year; risk-adjusted credits are 758,696.4 and 803,325.6 tCO2e/year. The result is not field validation or a forecast, and automation does not overcome constrained electricity, heat, or storage.

The case will include a cyclic storage boundary, explicit ordinary demand and additionality assumptions, a storage-injection and liability boundary, and a failure sensitivity for the automation premise. It is an analytical benchmark, not a recommendation to finance or build a facility.

## 5. Carbon, power, and storage work

The carbon baseline work draws on a published evolving RCMIP reference with a paired removal perturbation and matched controls ([Nicholls & Lewis, 2021](https://zenodo.org/records/4589756); [Nicholls et al., 2020](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/13/5175/2020/); [Joos et al., 2013](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/13/2793/2013/)). The initial absolute-output implementation is quarantined because its zero-future-emissions diagnostic is not trustworthy under large off-reference anomalies. The next output, if it resolves historical reservoir initialization, will be a hybrid conditional baseline rather than a calibrated Earth-system spin-up or validated forecast. It must isolate removal's marginal effect from changes in fossil emissions, methane, aerosols, and land use.

Power modeling must advance from representative profiles to regional 8760-hour weather, load, transmission, firming, maintenance, and additionality treatment. Storage work must move from pore-space proxies to basin-specific injection, pressure, permitting, monitoring, financial responsibility, and long-term liability. U.S. Class VI requirements and Subpart RR provide practical examples of the monitoring and financial obligations such a plan must confront ([U.S. EPA, 2026a](https://www.epa.gov/uic/class-vi-wells-used-geologic-sequestration-carbon-dioxide); [U.S. EPA, 2026b](https://www.epa.gov/ghgreporting/subpart-rr-geologic-sequestration-carbon-dioxide)).

## 6. Institutional and financial model

The public-carbon-service concept is an institutional hypothesis, not a preference for one political structure. Regulated operators, independent measurement, public procurement, private contractors, and regional compacts solve different coordination problems and create different conflicts. Operation, credit issuance, verification, target setting, and adjudication should not be concentrated without independent controls.

The service must separate current operations, capital, legacy drawdown, post-closure monitoring, and reversal reserves. The arithmetic makes the issue visible: at a 42.2 GtCO2/year emissions base, a $50/t fee raises about $2.11 trillion/year, while 100 GtCO2/year of removal at $50/t costs $5 trillion/year. That is an illustrative gap, not a policy prescription. It shows why historical drawdown, reserve funding, and a shrinking emissions-revenue base require an explicit model. Carbon takeback obligations provide one relevant precedent ([Jenkins et al., 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S254243512100489X)).

## 7. Limits and review questions

This program does not claim to reverse all climate harm. It cannot by itself settle methane, ocean acidification, biodiversity loss, hydrology, regional heat impacts, consent, or political authority. Approximately 280 ppm is a preindustrial reference range and long-horizon investigational restoration aspiration, not a demonstrated modern optimum, safe setpoint, or target date ([IPCC, 2021](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-2/)). Deliberate planetary atmospheric management is a limited literal form of terraforming; that label raises governance standards rather than proving controllability. Related capabilities could matter for closed habitats or other celestial bodies, but no readiness or transferability claim is made here.

The near-term review packet asks: Can the paired carbon baseline isolate removal? Can the regional case deliver additional power and required heat under cyclic storage? Can storage and MRV support the named liability? Does automation improve a measured bottleneck? Can the financial model survive under-delivery and declining fee revenue? A negative answer narrows or ends the relevant claim.
