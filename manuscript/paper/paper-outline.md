# AETHER Paper Outline

Working title: AETHER: Atmospheric Engineering Through High-Energy Removal

## 1. Introduction

- Frame climate reversal as a stock-flow and infrastructure scaling problem.
- Define what the paper means by climate reversal.
- Separate current insufficiency from future feasibility.

## 2. Background

- Atmospheric CO2 and emissions.
- Carbon-removal pathway categories.
- Energy and storage constraints.
- AI and robotics as possible physical-economy accelerators.

## 3. Research Question and Contribution

- State the feasibility-boundary question.
- Explain why existing discussions under-model automation, storage, and MRV.

## 4. Methods

- Source selection.
- Parameter database.
- Carbon stock-flow model.
- Removal cost-curve model.
- Robotics scaling model.
- Storage-state comparison.
- Commons ownership and citizen-compensation governance branch.
- Scenario design.

## 5. Results

Planned figures:

- CO2 trajectory by removal-capacity scenario.
- Energy demand by removal pathway.
- Cost-per-tonne sensitivity.
- Robot unit-cost and fleet-growth sensitivity.
- Storage pathway comparison.
- MRV failure-case impact.

## 6. Discussion

- Conditions where the abundance thesis becomes plausible.
- Conditions where it fails.
- Why durable storage and honest measurement are non-negotiable.
- Why robotics matters only if it reduces physical bottlenecks.
- Whether citizen-owned or trust-held planetary commons could change incentives around emissions, ocean use, and atmospheric dumping.

## 7. Limitations

- Model simplicity.
- Source uncertainty.
- Non-CO2 climate effects.
- Governance and political economy.
- Risks of turning commons payments into a permission slip for harm.
- Ecological and local environmental risks.

## 8. Conclusion

The conclusion should not repeat a motivational slogan. It should state the boundary conditions: what would have to be built, measured, powered, stored, and governed for CO2 cleanup to become real infrastructure.
